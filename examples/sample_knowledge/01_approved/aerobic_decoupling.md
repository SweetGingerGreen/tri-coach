# 核心摘要 (Summary)
Aerobic decoupling describes the gap between effort (pace or power) and heart rate during a steady-state aerobic session, and is a practical proxy for aerobic durability.

# 正文内容 (Content)
The classic decoupling test splits a steady aerobic session in half and compares the ratio of pace-to-heart-rate (or power-to-heart-rate) in the first half versus the second. If the second half shows a noticeably higher heart rate for the same effort, the athlete has "decoupled" — the cardiovascular system is working harder to maintain the same output. A common rule of thumb treats a drift of more than roughly 5% as a signal that aerobic base is not yet sufficient for the duration being attempted.

Several factors push decoupling higher and are worth disentangling before drawing conclusions: heat and dehydration, under-fueling on long rides, sleep debt, residual fatigue from the previous day's session, and altitude. A single high-drift session, especially in hot weather or after a poor night, is rarely meaningful on its own. The signal becomes useful when measured repeatedly under similar conditions and tracked over weeks.

Pa:HR and Pw:HR are the typical metrics on TrainingPeaks and similar platforms. Both compare normalized output to average heart rate across two halves of the session. They work best on steady, uninterrupted aerobic efforts; they are not reliable on interval workouts, races with surges, or sessions with significant elevation gain that distorts the second half.

For a long-course triathlete the practical question is durability: can you hold target race effort late in the race when accumulated fatigue is high? Drift trending downward over a base-building block, even if absolute fitness numbers move slowly, is one of the cleaner signs that the aerobic base is genuinely deepening.
