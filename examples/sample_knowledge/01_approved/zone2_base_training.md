# 核心摘要 (Summary)
Zone 2 base training builds aerobic capacity by riding or running at the upper edge of pure fat oxidation, typically below the first lactate threshold (LT1), for long, repeatable sessions.

# 正文内容 (Content)
Zone 2 is the intensity range where most fuel still comes from fat and blood lactate stays near baseline. Practically, this means a conversational pace where you can still speak in full sentences. For most amateur triathletes this falls between roughly 65% and 75% of FTP on the bike, or around 70% to 80% of lactate threshold heart rate on the run, but individual physiology varies and a structured ramp or lactate test gives a far better anchor than any percentage rule.

The training goal in Zone 2 is volume, not intensity. Sessions are long — 60 to 180 minutes is typical on the bike, 45 to 90 minutes on the run — and they accumulate week over week. Adaptations include increased mitochondrial density, better fat oxidation, improved capillary density, and a stronger aerobic base on top of which higher-intensity work eventually sits.

A common mistake is drifting up out of Zone 2 on long sessions, especially on hilly routes or when training in a group. Cardiac drift — heart rate climbing while power or pace holds steady — is normal late in a long Zone 2 session and is not in itself a reason to back off. However, if heart rate drifts more than about 5% relative to pace or power over the second half of a session compared to the first, that is a useful signal that aerobic durability still needs work.

Zone 2 is not the only intensity that matters, and an exclusively Zone 2 plan will eventually stop producing race-day improvements. It is, however, the largest single bucket of training in nearly every successful long-course triathlon plan, and protecting it from creeping intensity is one of the highest-leverage things a self-coached athlete can do.
